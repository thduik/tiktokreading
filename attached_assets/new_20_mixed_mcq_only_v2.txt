Title: Infrastructure Lock-In

Title: Bayesian Reasoning
Topic: Probability and Evidence

Bayesian reasoning treats evidence as something that updates prior beliefs rather than as information interpreted from nowhere. A test result, for example, means different things depending on how common the condition was before the test and how reliable the test is. This does not make evidence subjective in the sense of arbitrary opinion; it makes interpretation conditional on assumptions that should be stated. The method matters because it turns uncertainty into a process of revision rather than a demand for instant certainty.

Title: Judicial Precedent
Topic: Law and Institutions

Judicial precedent allows earlier court decisions to guide later cases, but it is not mechanical repetition. Title: Moral Licensing
Topic: Psychology and Behaviour

Moral licensing describes a pattern in which people who have done something they see as good may feel permitted to act less carefully afterward. The point is not that good actions cause bad behaviour automatically, but that self-image can change how later choices are justified. This makes the phenomenon difficult to study, because motivation, context, and social norms shape whether licensing appears. Its importance lies in showing that ethical behaviour depends not only on values, but also on how people narrate their own virtue.
