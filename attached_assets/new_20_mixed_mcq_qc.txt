Title: The Precautionary PrincipleTopic: Policy and Risk

The precautionary principle is often misunderstood as a demand to avoid all risk, but its sharper role is to guide action when possible harm is serious and evidence remains incomplete. It shifts part of the burden from proving damage after the fact to justifying safety before irreversible consequences occur. Yet applying it requires judgment, because excessive caution can also delay beneficial technologies or necessary interventions. The principle matters because it makes uncertainty politically active rather than allowing uncertainty to become an excuse for inaction.
